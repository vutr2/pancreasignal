# Results

> Generated by `python scripts/make_results.py` from `reports/` (newest run per task and model).
> Do not edit by hand: rerun the script. Research prototype, not for clinical use.

## Setup

- **Data:** Debernardi et al. 2020 urine panel, 590 samples (file hash in `data/manifest.json`).
- **Features:** LYVE1, REG1B, TFF1 divided by urine creatinine, then `log1p`, plus age and sex. The age-only baseline uses age alone (`configs/urine_age_only.yaml`).
- **Models:** logistic regression (scaler inside the Pipeline), XGBoost, each with and without isotonic calibration (`CalibratedClassifierCV`, 5 inner folds, fitted on the training fold only).
- **Splits:** PDAC vs benign and PDAC vs rest use leave-one-center-out (test hospitals BPTB, LIV, ESP; UCL has no PDAC and is skipped). PDAC vs control uses random stratified 5-fold because every control comes from BPTB; its test patients come from the same hospitals as its training patients, so it is optimistic.
- **Metrics:** on out-of-fold predictions. AUROC 95% CI and paired differences by bootstrap (1000 resamples, seed 42). Sensitivity at 90% / 95% specificity uses one threshold for all hospitals.

## Key findings

1. **Logistic regression is the best model.** Across hospitals it reaches AUROC 0.788 (0.742–0.831) for PDAC vs benign and 0.785 (0.745–0.823) for PDAC vs rest. XGBoost is lower by +0.041 (+0.010 to +0.071) on the same patients; the gap comes from the BPTB fold (+0.052 (+0.008 to +0.098)), while on patients from the training hospitals (PDAC vs control, 5-fold) they tie (-0.000 (-0.017 to +0.015)).
2. **The biomarkers beat age alone at BPTB but not at LIV.** Inside the BPTB fold, logistic regression beats the age-only model by +0.097 (+0.044 to +0.147) (benign) and +0.135 (+0.088 to +0.183) (rest), both CIs exclude 0. At LIV the difference is +0.009 (-0.068 to +0.085) (benign) and +0.000 (-0.081 to +0.080) (rest). Every benign patient at LIV has pancreatitis, the hardest condition to tell apart from PDAC.
3. **One risk scale does not transfer between hospitals.** The share of PDAC differs a lot by hospital (20% at BPTB vs 70% at LIV in PDAC vs rest), and each fold model learns the PDAC rate of the hospitals it trained on. The logistic regression predicts an average risk of 0.43 at BPTB (true rate 0.20) and 0.47 at LIV (true rate 0.70). The age-only model is hit hardest: pooled AUROC 0.512 for PDAC vs rest, although inside each hospital it ranks patients reasonably (see section 2). A real deployment would need recalibration on local data.
4. **Calibration helps XGBoost, not logistic regression.** Isotonic calibration changes the ECE for PDAC vs benign from 0.167 to 0.090 for XGBoost (its raw probabilities are far from the observed rates) but from 0.066 to 0.071 for logistic regression, which is already close to calibrated. The calibrator is fitted on the training hospitals, so it cannot fix the between-hospital shift from finding 3.
5. **Plasma CA19-9 is better on the same patients.** PDAC vs benign: AUROC 0.890 vs 0.780; at 95% specificity CA19-9 finds 73% of cancers, the urine model 26%.
6. **Early-stage cancers are found about as often as late ones** (PDAC vs benign at 90% specificity: stage I–II 0.47 of 102, III–IV 0.40 of 97). But most I–II cases are IIB, and only 16 patients are stage I.

Different patients: 571 serum samples, 2555 miRNAs (Toray 3D-Gene array). No hospital information, so random stratified 5-fold. Elastic-net logistic regression with nested CV: the detection filter, scaling, and the choice of penalty all happen inside the training fold.

### Findings

1. **Two tasks look perfect, one is at chance.** AUROC 1.000 for PDAC vs control and 0.999 for PDAC vs benign, but 0.520 for PDAC vs biliary tract cancer, where the inner CV kept no miRNA at all in 4 of 5 folds.
2. **The perfect scores are probably not about cancer.** A single miRNA separates healthy controls from every other group, a PDAC-vs-control model calls most benign and other-cancer samples "PDAC", and even two batches of healthy controls can be told apart almost perfectly. The only groups that do not separate are pancreatic and biliary cancer, which seem to have been collected together. The most likely explanation is a collection or processing batch effect. Evidence: [notebooks/03_mirna.ipynb](notebooks/03_mirna.ipynb).
3. **Age and sex alone** reach AUROC 0.723 for PDAC vs control (the controls are 91% male).
4. **GEO quantile-normalised all 571 samples together** before release, so test samples influenced the normalisation of training samples. It uses no labels, but it is one more reason to read these numbers as optimistic.

### Caveats

- The 50 questions were written from the corpus by someone who had read it, so they reuse its wording: retrieval numbers are optimistic. Real users phrase questions differently.
- The answer pipeline (prompt, JSON schema, verifier, number check) was improved over five runs on THESE 50 questions, so the final numbers are tuned to them, like tuning on a test set. A fresh, held-out set of questions is needed for an unbiased estimate.
- Faithfulness and key-point coverage are graded by an LLM, not by a clinician.
- 'Fully correct' is strict: key points come from one gold article, so an answer that uses another article with a different figure (e.g. 'up to 15%' instead of 'about 8%' of cancers from IPMN/MCN) counts as wrong.
- No separate reranker: the NVIDIA reranking endpoints were end-of-life; hybrid search fuses vector and BM25 with RRF.
- Kyoto 2024 IPMN guidelines and CAPS 2020 are not in the corpus (license / availability), so answers cannot use them.

## Limitations

- **Case-control data**, not a screening population: PDAC is 34% here, far below 1% in real screening.
- **Hospitals differ in who they sent**: all controls from BPTB, all LIV/ESP/UCL benign patients have pancreatitis. Some of what any model learns may be hospital, not disease.
- **Small folds**: ESP has 29 patients; sensitivity numbers have no CI yet.
- **CA19-9 subset** is not random (missing not at random), so the head-to-head covers part of the data only.

## Sanity checks

- Highest AUROC anywhere: 0.929 (> 0.97 would suggest leakage).
- PDAC vs control (0.929) > PDAC vs benign (0.788), as expected (different splits, rough check only).
- miRNA: highest AUROC 1.000, above 0.97, so it was investigated (section 8, notebook 03): no code leakage (shuffled labels give ~0.5), but a strong batch effect.

for t in pdac_vs_benign pdac_vs_rest; do
  python -m pancreasignal.train --task $t
  python -m pancreasignal.train --task $t --calibrate
  python -m pancreasignal.train --task $t --model xgboost
  python -m pancreasignal.train --task $t --model xgboost --calibrate
  python -m pancreasignal.train --task $t --config configs/urine_age_only.yaml
done
# pdac_vs_control: same five commands with --split stratified_kfold
for t in pdac_vs_control pdac_vs_biliary pdac_vs_benign; do
  python -m pancreasignal.train --config configs/mirna.yaml --task $t
  python -m pancreasignal.train --config configs/mirna_demographics.yaml --task $t
done

python scripts/make_results.py
